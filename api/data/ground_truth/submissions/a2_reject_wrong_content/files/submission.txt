Introduction
This midterm report provides a comprehensive summary of the key concepts and theories covered during the first seven weeks of the Organizational Behavior course. The primary objective of this document is to synthesize the learning outcomes regarding individual behavior, group dynamics, and leadership structures within modern organizations.

Individual Behavior and Perception
The initial weeks focused heavily on the individual level of analysis. We examined personality traits using the Big Five model, which remains a critical framework for understanding employee potential and job fit. Furthermore, the module on perception and attribution theory highlighted how managers often misinterpret employee actions due to cognitive biases. Understanding these psychological underpinnings is essential for effective human resource management. The course material emphasized that perception is not reality, and that subjective interpretation drives decision-making processes in the workplace. Self-awareness is a key component of professional development.

Group Dynamics and Team Effectiveness
Moving into the middle section of the term, the curriculum shifted toward group behavior. We analyzed the stages of group development, specifically forming, storming, norming, and performing. The distinction between formal and informal groups was also explored, noting how informal networks often influence productivity more than official structures. Team cohesion and conflict management were central themes. We learned that task conflict can be beneficial for innovation, whereas relationship conflict is almost always detrimental to team performance. Effective communication remains the cornerstone of successful collaboration.

Leadership and Organizational Culture
The final weeks of the covered material addressed leadership theories and organizational culture. Transformational leadership was contrasted with transactional leadership, with evidence suggesting transformational leaders drive higher employee engagement. A strong culture can act as a control mechanism, reducing the need for strict supervision. However, cultural misalignment during mergers was identified as a significant risk factor. This dynamic environment requires adaptable management approaches.
